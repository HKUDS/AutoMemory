## 2025-W24
- Apple's WWDC failed to excite investors, with its Liquid Glass interface drawing criticism and the stock lacking a catalyst, highlighting ongoing innovation challenges.
- The company is defending its AI strategy as Apple Intelligence integrates into core apps, but key Siri features remain delayed, raising questions about its competitive pace.
- Oracle's forecast for over 70% cloud infrastructure sales growth signals strong momentum in enterprise cloud spending, benefiting the broader sector.
- Amazon's nuclear energy deal for data centers addresses power cost and sustainability concerns, supporting its AWS expansion amid rising AI-driven electricity demand.
- Despite job cuts, Amazon is viewed as a tech leader due to strength in AWS, advertising, and ventures like Project Kuiper, underscoring its diversified growth.
- Applied Optoelectronics faces significant cash burn and potential dilution from high capital expenditures, reflecting pressure in some tech hardware segments.
- The focus on data center power agreements and cloud forecasts points to sustained infrastructure investment, a tailwind for semiconductor and utility sectors.
- Global tech remains bifurcated, with cloud and AI leaders like Oracle and Amazon showing strength, while consumer-facing innovation at Apple struggles for market approval.
- Central bank policies impacting capital costs are a key risk for tech firms with high capex, as seen in cash flow challenges at hardware suppliers.
